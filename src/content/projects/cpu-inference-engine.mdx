## Problem

CPU decode needs to turn compute into tokens without leaving bandwidth on the table. Prefill and decode sit on opposite sides of the roofline: prefill amortizes weight reads across many tokens, while decode rereads every weight for every token.

## Approach

A from-scratch C++17 Llama engine with separate `prefill` and `decode` paths, AVX2 kernels, OpenMP threading, and GGUF models. Decode is scored against a measured read-only STREAM ceiling for the machine, not a datasheet peak.

## Key decisions

SIMD and threading help prefill more than decode, because faster compute saturates memory with fewer threads. Cache blocking did not help decode and hurt short-prompt prefill. A mixed Q4 GGUF decoded slower than Q8_0 because Q4_1 and Q6_K tensors fell back to a scalar dequant path and left the memory-bound regime.

## Results

Best public figure: 9.3x decode on Llama 3.2 1B Q8_0 versus the scalar single-thread baseline, at 73% of the STREAM ceiling. Across the reported models, decode reached 56 to 73% of that ceiling.
